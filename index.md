## How an impulse sealer works

An impulse sealer heats its wire only while you press the arm down. There is no warm-up and no element that stays hot on the counter. You set the timer dial, lay the pouch mouth flat on the bar, press the arm until the red light goes off, then hold for about two seconds so the seam sets. The result is a 2 mm wide seal across the bag.

## Which bags fit an 8 inch sealer

The bar is 200 mm long, so the widest pouch mouth it handles is about 7.5 inch when laid flat. That covers:

- **4 x 6 inch pouches** for tea, spices, seeds and dry fruit samples
- **6 x 9 inch poly bags** for medicines, hardware parts, jewellery and cosmetics
- **7 x 10 inch pouches** for 100 to 250 g of namkeen or candy
- **Zip-lock pouches** narrower than 7.5 inch, sealed just below the zip

It is not the right size for an 8 x 10 inch bag (the mouth is wider than the bar), garment bags or 5 to 10 kg rice and atta sacks. For those, PackForge suggests the [10 inch](https://www.packforge.co.in/impulse-sealer-10-inch.html), [12 inch](https://www.packforge.co.in/impulse-sealer-12-inch.html) or [20 inch impulse sealer](https://www.packforge.co.in/impulse-sealer-20-inch.html). Plain foil and metallised pouches need a hot bar sealer instead; the [impulse vs hot bar sealer comparison](https://www.packforge.co.in/impulse-vs-hot-bar-sealer.html) explains the difference.

## Dial settings that work

![Hand impulse sealer in use on a shop counter](https://www.packforge.co.in/assets/img/products/hand-impulse-sealer-2.webp)

- **Dial 1 to 3:** thin seed and sample film
- **Dial 3 to 5:** regular PE tea and masala pouches
- **Higher settings:** thicker PP or PE-lined laminate pouches

Start low and step up one level at a time. A seal that peels apart needs more heat; a seal that turns brown or burns through needs less. The [impulse sealer troubleshooting guide](https://www.packforge.co.in/impulse-sealer-troubleshooting.html) covers weak and burnt seals in more detail.

## Should you buy the 8 inch size?

Choose the 8 inch model if most of your pouches are 7 inch wide or narrower and you pack small quantities through the day. If you also pack 500 g or 1 kg bags, a 10 or 12 inch bar saves you from buying a second machine later.
